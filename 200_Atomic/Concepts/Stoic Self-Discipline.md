# Stoic Self-Discipline

## Core Idea

Six principles from Marcus Aurelius for cultivating self-discipline through detachment from external outcomes and emotional reactions.

## Why It Matters

Modern life is full of distractions, desires, and disappointments. Stoic discipline isn't about suppression—it's about directing energy toward what you can control and releasing what you can't.

## Key Points

### The Six Principles

1. **Don't grieve separation** - Accept endings without suffering
2. **Resentment and complaints are inappropriate** - Neither to yourself nor others
3. **Don't surrender to love and lust** - Maintain rationality in passion
4. **Don't expect** - Expect the unexpected; what you want rarely arrives as planned
5. **Don't chase delicious food** - Resist hedonic adaptation and luxury
6. **Don't hold what you no longer need** - Let go of obsolete attachments

## Examples

### Example 1: Project Failure
**Non-stoic:** Resentment, blame team, spiral in regret
**Stoic:**
- Accept: Project failed (Principle 1)
- No complaints: "team was incompetent" (Principle 2)
- No expectation: "next one will definitely work" (Principle 4)
- Release: Let go of failed approach (Principle 6)
- Move forward with lessons

### Example 2: Relationship Dynamics
**Non-stoic:** "If they truly cared, they would..."
**Stoic:**
- Don't expect specific behavior (Principle 4)
- Don't grieve if they leave (Principle 1)
- Don't lose rationality in love (Principle 3)

### Example 3: Material Desires
**Non-stoic:** Must have latest tech, fancy meals, luxury items
**Stoic:**
- Don't chase gourmet experiences (Principle 5)
- Let go of unused possessions (Principle 6)
- Find contentment in simple, functional tools

## How to Apply

**Daily practice:**

Morning premeditatio malorum:
- What might go wrong today?
- How will I respond with equanimity?
- What am I expecting that I should release?

Evening review:
- Where did I complain today? (Principle 2)
- What separation did I resist? (Principle 1)
- What am I holding I don't need? (Principle 6)

**For specific challenges:**

| Situation | Stoic Response |
|---|---|
| Argument brewing | Principle 2: No complaints/resentment |
| Desire for upgrade | Principle 5: Don't chase luxury |
| Relationship tension | Principle 4: Release expectations |
| Delayed project | Principle 1: Don't grieve setbacks |
| Clutter accumulation | Principle 6: Release what's obsolete |

**Integration with modern life:**

Principle 4 (Don't expect) + Agile: Ship MVPs, expect user needs to differ
Principle 6 (Let go) + Refactoring: Delete dead code without attachment
Principle 2 (No complaints) + Retrospectives: Facts over blame
