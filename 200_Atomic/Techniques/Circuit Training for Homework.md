# Circuit Training for Homework

## Core Idea

Alternate 15-30 minute study sessions with sets of physical exercise to maximize learning efficiency, prevent mental fatigue, and maintain energy.

## Why It Matters

Combines neuroplasticity benefits of exercise with focused study. Physical activity increases blood flow to brain, improves memory consolidation, and prevents the energy crashes of marathon study sessions.

## Key Points

- **Interval structure** - 15-30 min study + 1 exercise set
- **Subject rotation** - Different topics each interval (interleaving)
- **Physical exercise** - Bodyweight exercises (push-ups, sit-ups, squats, etc.)
- **Energy maintenance** - Prevents mental fatigue accumulation
- **Memory consolidation** - Exercise between sessions improves retention
- **Sustainable** - Can study longer without burnout

## Examples

### Example 1: Evening Study Session
- 15 min: Algebra problems
- 1 set: 20 push-ups
- 15 min: German vocabulary
- 1 set: 30 sit-ups
- 15 min: History reading
- 1 set: 15 squats
- 15 min: Programming practice
- 1 set: Plank 60 sec

**Result:** 60 min quality study + full bodyweight workout

### Example 2: Preparing for Exams
3-hour study block becomes:
- 6 study intervals (different subjects)
- 6 exercise sets
- Never feel "brain fog"
- Body workout as bonus

## How to Apply

**Setup:**
1. Choose 3-4 subjects to study
2. Prepare exercise space (no equipment needed)
3. Set timer for 15-minute intervals
4. Have water nearby

**Execution:**
1. Study ONE topic for 15-30 min (focused, no distractions)
2. Immediately do 1 exercise set (30-60 seconds)
3. 30-second rest, drink water
4. Switch to DIFFERENT topic
5. Repeat 4-6 cycles

**Exercise variety:**
- Upper body: Push-ups, dips, arm circles
- Core: Sit-ups, planks, leg raises
- Lower body: Squats, lunges, calf raises
- Cardio: Jumping jacks, burpees, high knees

**Key rule:** Break must be SHORT. It's not rest—it's active recovery that maintains alertness.
