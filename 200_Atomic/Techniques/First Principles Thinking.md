# First Principles Thinking

## Core Idea

Breaking down complex problems to their fundamental truths and reasoning up from there, rather than reasoning by analogy.

## Why It Matters

Most people solve problems by copying what others do (reasoning by analogy). First principles thinking lets you innovate, optimize, and truly understand problems by questioning every assumption and rebuilding from foundational truths.

## Key Points

- Deconstruct problems into their most basic elements
- Question all assumptions, even those "everyone believes"
- Each component must be analyzed independently
- Rebuild solutions from fundamental truths
- Creates opportunity for innovation and optimization
- Enables teaching others more effectively

## Examples

### Example 1: Writing Essays
Essay → Paragraphs → Sentences → Words → Letters

To master essay writing, you must first master each component separately. Only then can you say you truly understand the craft.

### Example 2: Software Architecture
Instead of copying another team's microservices architecture (analogy), ask:
- What problems are we actually solving?
- What are the fundamental constraints (latency, scale, team size)?
- What's the simplest architecture that addresses these?

## How to Apply

1. **Identify the problem or situation** - Understand and break down the problem clearly
2. **Question assumptions** - Apply critical thinking. Doubt basic "facts" everyone believes. Ask why things work the way they do
3. **Break it down to basics** - Decompose to fundamental components
4. **Analyze each component** - Check what each piece does, why it's needed, seek alternatives
5. **Generate creative solutions** - Think outside the box using your understanding of basics
6. **Test and evaluate** - Validate your new approach
7. **Iterate and refine** - Don't be afraid to step back and start over
8. **Apply to different areas** - Don't limit yourself to one domain
