# Pomodoro Technique

## Core Idea

Work in focused 25-minute intervals followed by 5-minute breaks to maintain energy, motivation, and prevent mental fatigue.

## Why It Matters

Prevents burnout, maintains high quality attention, and makes large tasks feel manageable by breaking them into discrete time boxes. The breaks are not optional—they're essential for sustained performance.

## Key Points

- **25 minutes of focused work** - Single task, full attention
- **5 minutes of complete break** - MUST fully disconnect from work
- **Prevents mental fatigue** - Short sprints vs. marathon
- **Maintains motivation** - Frequent small wins
- **Break quality matters** - Don't just "rest", completely switch context

## Examples

### Example 1: Studying
- 25 min: Math problems
- 5 min: Walk around, look out window
- 25 min: History reading
- 5 min: Stretch, water
Notice: Each pomodoro = different subject (interleaving)

### Example 2: Coding
- 25 min: Implement feature
- 5 min: Stand up, coffee, no screens
- 25 min: Write tests
- 5 min: Brief walk

After 4 pomodoros: 15-30 min longer break

## How to Apply

1. **Choose one task** - No multitasking during pomodoro
2. **Set timer for 25 minutes** - Physical timer better than phone
3. **Work until timer rings** - No breaking early
4. **Take 5-minute break** - COMPLETELY disconnect, no checking phone/email
5. **After 4 pomodoros** - Take longer 15-30 min break

**Break activities:**
- Physical movement (walk, stretch)
- Look at distant objects (rest eyes)
- Hydrate
- Brief meditation

**Avoid during breaks:**
- Social media
- Email
- Continuing work "just a bit longer"
